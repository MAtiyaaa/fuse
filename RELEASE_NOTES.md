# Fuse 0.2.7 - The Swap & Clean Update

## New

- **Menus below.** On a handheld with two screens (the AYN Thor), Fuse's menus can move to the
  touch screen, and the main screen shows what they have chosen, large, like a 3DS: the game's art
  over the whole screen, its logo, what it is, a few lines about it, your time played and its
  achievements, with the game being played first and how long this session has run. With nothing
  chosen it shows the time and the games you played last. Choose it in setup, in Settings, Screen
  and sound, Which way round, or with Swap screens in the quick menu. The controller works as
  always, and where the touch screen doesn't take Fuse's menus they stay on the main screen.
- **Fuseline, Fuse's own animation engine.** Every animation in Fuse now runs on code of its own,
  named after the line of a fuse in its logo: its curves, springs, fades, slides, loops, scrolling
  and choreography. Everything moves exactly as it did, and new animations build on it.
- **A first opening for setup.** The very first start of Fuse opens with a longer animation of its
  own: a spark runs along the fuse line, the camera pulls back as the frame draws itself, the mark
  lights in a flash, and the name burns in beside it. Any button skips it.
- **Which way round?** Setup asks it on a device with two screens, with a drawing of the device
  whose screens swap as you move between Menus on top, Menus below and One screen.
- **Release notes get a page.** Settings, About, "What's new in this version" opens this version's
  notes as a page of their own, kept inside Fuse so they work offline: the version and its name,
  then New, Changed and Fixed as cards, read with the D-pad and paged with the triggers. An update
  on offer opens its notes the same way.
- **Undo and Reset while arranging Home.** A small bar at the top right, reached by moving up past
  the top row or by touch: Undo takes back the last change, as many times as you like, and Reset
  (after asking) puts Home back as it came.
- **The second screen follows the main background.** The theme's scene or your own picture now
  shows behind the second screen too, so both screens look like one device. Settings, Screen and
  sound, Same background as the main screen turns it off.

## Changed

- The line under the top tabs stays exactly under its tab while the tabs move, and glides only
  when you go to another one.
- The setup logo is Fuse's real mark, drawn in from its own lines, and the controller test is a
  whole controller drawn in your theme: its bumpers and triggers named the way your pad names them
  (LB and LT, L1 and L2, L and ZL), two sticks, a D-pad, the face buttons (with PlayStation's shapes
  on a PlayStation pad), and Fuse's mark as its guide button. Every button lights as you press it.
- Setup's Flow and Channels pictures look like the real Homes: Flow's stage over a row of wide
  cards and a row of systems, and Channels as the board it starts with.
- The Library shrinks far less as it scrolls: the stage folds to one line, as on a system's page,
  and the tiles keep almost their full size.
- A game's "On this device" cards sit three in a row on the Thor, all in view.
- The "2 by 2" label while dragging or resizing a widget is gone.
- On a device without a second screen, nothing in Fuse mentions one: no setup step, no settings,
  no quick menu tile, and the Store picks the right edition of an app by itself.

## Fixed

- Up reaches the top line from every page with one, including a system's games, Settings and
  Search, and Down goes back to the page.
- Right at the edge of Search's keyboard no longer jumps into the results; they stay a press of
  Menu away.
- Holding the controller's button on the keyboard's Delete key deletes more than one letter,
  faster the longer it is held, as holding Backspace does.
- The time played on a game page can be reached with the D-pad, and opens Play time.
- In setup, Up and Down move between buttons that wrap onto a second line.
