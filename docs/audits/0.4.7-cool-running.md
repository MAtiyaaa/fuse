# Fuse 0.4.7: The Cool Running Update

Engineering record for 0.4.7: what was found by reading the interface's code against the frame
times, heat and battery reported on an AYN Thor (Library 111 to 200 ms a frame, Home 226 ms, tab
changes 263 ms, Systems 90 to 160 ms, Jellyfin 111 to 127 ms, Android Ports 108 ms, the processor at
86 degrees and the battery down 8 points in three minutes, the Motion Inspector watching 920 values
while moving through Systems), what was changed, how it was checked, and what is not done. Every
number here comes from runs on one Linux container drawing in software; nothing was measured on a
handheld. Software drawing makes absolute frame times meaningless (99.6 percent of the drawing
thread's time was Skia filling pixels on the processor), so changes were judged by the work they
remove, counted directly, not by frame times from this machine.

The rule for every change: the same motion or more. Nothing was made to move less.

## 1. Causes found and changed

| Cause (found by reading) | Consequence | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Library read the grid's selected index at the top of the screen | Every step through the games composed the whole page again: header, prefetch, stage, the grid's own lambda | The index is read only where it is used: the stage, the cover carousel, prefetch and the room each read it in a small scope of their own (`SelectedStage`, `SelectedIndex`, `LibraryPrefetch`, `LibraryRoom`); the key handlers read it when a key comes |
| The top line read the room's art to decide its scrim | The whole top line (clock, battery, tabs) composed again on every step anywhere | `TopScrim` reads whether there is art through `derivedStateOf`: only a change between art and none reaches it |
| The second screen followed every step of the selection at once, and the room and the page each built their own game detail (about a dozen database flows and a launch resolution each) | Two new detail pipelines per step, a decode per step, a slide per step, on the same thread that draws the main screen | `rememberCalmFocus`: a quick run of presses is followed once it rests (140 ms after a press that came within 140 ms of the one before; a single press follows at once). `CompanionDetails`: one detail per game shared by the room and the page, kept four seconds after the last reader and four games deep, so moving back is instant |
| Every board widget restarted four position animations each time its page was shown (a `PageEffect`) | Hundreds of animations on a page of systems for widgets already in place | `boardPlace` runs from a `LaunchedEffect` keyed on the place, and does nothing when the widget is already there and still |
| The board's geometry was a new object on every composition | Every widget composed again whenever the board did | `BoardGeometry` is immutable and remembered by what it is made from |
| On a short screen, the Systems header folding away changed the board's height on every frame of the fold, and the page read the fold at its top | The whole Systems page and the whole board (every widget) composed on every frame of the fold, and the fill rule ran its layouts each frame | The fold is read only while the header is laid out (`foldAway(() -> Float)`); the board composes again only when its shape changes (`BoardFrame` over `BoardShape`: width, narrow, small, columns), never for its height alone |
| Each tile with art waited 400 ms and then set a "slow" flag, and its size, once measured, changed its request | Every tile composed again 400 ms after it appeared, and asked for its art twice (the second request cancelled the first) | The slow fallback is skipped once art arrived; the decode size grows only when a tile outgrows what was decoded, so a tile's first request stands |
| The theme's moving room drew on, thirty times a second, under a game's art that covered it completely | A whole screen drawn again every frame, on each screen, behind opaque art, whenever Fuse rested on a game | `HeroCover`: the backdrop says when its art covers everything; the room rests while it does (where glass lets it show through, it keeps moving). Nothing visible changes |
| Jellyfin's logos were asked for at one size by the prefetch and another by the second screen | Every logo fetched and decoded twice; the second screen waited on its own fetch | One size for logos everywhere (`JellyfinImages.LOGO_PX`) |
| The Android Ports page put its header, a stage and its filters above a grid of box art on a 360 dp tall screen | On the Thor's upper screen the grid began below the hints: almost none of it in view | Rebuilt (see the release notes): the heading folds away as you move or scroll, tiles shrink a fifth, and short screens get slim cards, three rows in view |

## 2. The 920 watched values

The Motion Inspector counts values in motion. A probe (`MotionProbe.kt`, run with
`./gradlew :ui:shell:desktopAudit -Pfuse.audit.dir=<dir> -Pfuse.audit.only=probe --tests '*UiAudit.probeL'`)
builds a library of 95 systems (a folder for every console and handheld Fuse knows), opens Systems
at the Thor's lower screen size and moves through it, then the Library, Home and the tabs, counting
the values in motion every frame after each press.

| Run | Systems, right | Systems, down | Library, down | Home, right | Changing tabs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before (0.4.6.1) | 18 | 11 | 17 | 14 | 391 |
| After (0.4.7) | 18 | 11 | 17 | 14 | 36 |

Each figure is the most values in motion at once in that run, the same probe over the same library
on both. Moving within a page was already lean. The spike was showing a page with a board: every
widget on it (95 systems, four values each) started moving to the place it was already in. With
more systems on a page, four values for each is the 920 the Thor showed. In 0.4.7 a widget already
in place does nothing.

## 3. Checked

- Compiled for the desktop; `:core:data` settings tests (with a new one for each person's own
  emulator), `:core:romm` tests (with a new one for telling copies apart by size).
- The second screen while playing, its Journal and the notebook, Android Ports with a port's page,
  and the Library's All page with other devices' filters, rendered at the AYN Thor's upper screen
  (J, 640 by 360 dp), its lower screen (L, 496 by 432 dp) and a tall second screen (C), and looked
  at. What the renders caught and was fixed: the Journal's heading came to rest behind the status
  line; the page dots sat on the notebook's text; a narrow screen gave Android Ports three huge
  covers and one row (it takes the cards now); a port's page squeezed its case and its details side
  by side on a narrow screen (its buttons go under the case now); the list and the port's page ran
  their foot under the hints; and starting to write took the session page's scrolling away, which
  would have sent it back to its top (the finger stops scrolling it now, nothing more).
- Two pages read their scroll position while composing, so every frame of a scroll composed the
  whole page again (a port's page, a Store app's page): their edges follow the scroll state now.

## 4. Not done

- Nothing was measured on a Thor. Frame times, heat and battery need the device.
- The grain over a moving room is still two overlay passes a frame where the room moves and shows.
  Making it cheaper without changing how it looks needs measuring on the device's graphics card.
- Art-less tiles still lay out their generated art with a subcomposition each.
